Five unforgettable days on the French and Italian Riviera

From the Belle Époque elegance of Nice to the pastel lanes of Menton, then across the border into Liguria for gardens, harbours, focaccia and the old streets of Sanremo. Here is a five-day route that lets two countries feel like one beautiful coast.

A practical, independent itinerary by Inside Riviera · France and Italy

The French and Italian Rivieras meet at the edge of the Mediterranean, but each side has its own rhythm. France brings grand promenades, hilltop villages and colourful old towns; just across the border, western Liguria feels a little more intimate, with flower-growing towns, olive groves, lively markets and family-run trattorias. This itinerary follows the coast from Nice to Imperia, taking in the eastern French Riviera and the Italian Riviera dei Fiori. It is designed for five full days, with enough time to enjoy each stop rather than simply tick it off.

Day 1 · Nice: old-town lanes and the Baie des Anges

Begin in one of the Riviera’s great cities. Nice is a rewarding introduction, with a lively market, grand seafront and a historic centre best explored on foot.

Morning
Vieux Nice · Cours Saleya

Start with the old town and its market

Ease into the trip with a wander through Vieux Nice. Its narrow streets are lined with ochre and terracotta façades, small food shops, churches and cafés. Head to Cours Saleya for the market atmosphere and a first taste of the region: seasonal fruit, olives, local cheeses, flowers and socca, the thin chickpea pancake cooked in a wood-fired oven.

Make time for: Place Rossetti and the Cathédrale Sainte-Réparate, then pause for a coffee or a slice of pissaladière.

Local tip: The market schedule changes by day and season. Check the current arrangements before planning your morning around a particular stall.
Afternoon
Promenade des Anglais · Castle Hill

Walk the seafront and climb to the viewpoint

Follow the curve of the Promenade des Anglais beside the blue water, then make your way up to Colline du Château. The panoramic view takes in the Baie des Anges, the rooftops of the old town and the port. You can return through the old streets or continue towards the harbour for a change of scene.

Evening
Nice · Dinner

Stay for a Niçoise dinner

Choose a small restaurant in or around the old town and look for salade niçoise, petits farcis, ravioli niçois or daube. For something casual, share a pan bagnat or socca. Keep the evening relaxed: tomorrow is a scenic day along the coast.

Day 2 · Èze, Monaco and Menton: three Riviera moods

This is the most ambitious day of the itinerary. Start early, choose your priorities and use the train for the coastal legs. Èze sits above the railway, so it needs a bus, taxi or organised transfer.

Morning
Èze Village

Explore the stone lanes of Èze

Perched high above the sea, Èze is a maze of cobbled passages, stone houses, galleries and viewpoints. Wander slowly and, if it interests you, visit the exotic garden at the top of the village for its dramatic coastal panorama. The village is steep and can become busy, so an early arrival makes a difference.

Getting there: Èze village is not the same as Èze-sur-Mer station. Plan the uphill connection in advance and check bus times; a taxi can save time on a tight itinerary.
Midday
Monaco · Monte-Carlo

See Monaco’s harbour and historic Rock

Continue east to Monaco. Take in Port Hercule, the elegant streets around Monte-Carlo and, if time allows, the old town on Le Rocher with its palace square and cathedral. Monaco is compact but has steep climbs and plenty to see, so focus on a walk rather than trying to visit every attraction.

Choose one extra: The Oceanographic Museum is a memorable option if you have several hours; otherwise enjoy the harbour and architecture from outside.

Late day
Menton · Old town and seafront

Finish in Menton, the lemon-scented border town

Menton is a lovely place to slow down. Its pastel old town rises above the harbour, with stairways, narrow lanes and views towards Italy. Walk the waterfront, explore the old basilica area and look for local lemon products in the shops. If daylight allows, stroll through one of the town’s celebrated gardens.

Overnight: Stay in Menton for a gentle start to the border-crossing day, or return to Nice if you prefer not to change hotels.

Day 3 · Cross into Italy: Ventimiglia and Bordighera

Today the border crossing is part of the pleasure. The architecture, food and pace begin to shift as you enter Liguria, while the Mediterranean remains your constant companion.

Morning
Ventimiglia · Italy

Discover Ventimiglia’s old town and market life

Take the coastal train from Menton towards Ventimiglia. Start in the lower town near the seafront and station, then cross towards Ventimiglia Alta, the historic quarter above the Roya River. Its lanes, arches and viewpoints feel a world away from the modern waterfront. If your visit falls on a market day, explore the stalls and food shops, but check the current schedule before setting out.

For lunch: Try farinata, focaccia, fresh pasta or seafood. This is a good place to begin noticing the differences between Niçoise and Ligurian cooking.

Afternoon
Bordighera · Old town and gardens

Spend a slow afternoon in Bordighera

A short train ride brings you to Bordighera. Explore the medieval upper town, Bordighera Alta, then head down towards the palm-lined seafront. The town has a gentler, garden-filled character, with historic villas and subtropical planting that thrive in its mild climate. If you have time, visit the area around the gardens associated with the English landscape tradition and Claude Monet’s visits.

Local tip: The old town is uphill from the station and waterfront. Wear comfortable shoes and leave time for the walk between the two.
Evening
Bordighera or Sanremo · Dinner

Choose a seaside dinner and settle into Liguria

Stay overnight in Bordighera for a quieter evening, or continue to Sanremo if you want a larger choice of hotels and restaurants. Order local olive oil, Taggiasca olives, pesto, trofie or seafood, and finish with a stroll along the waterfront.

Day 4 · Dolceacqua and Sanremo: medieval Liguria meets the seaside

Pair one of the most atmospheric inland villages in western Liguria with an afternoon in the region’s best-known resort town.

Morning
Dolceacqua · Nervia Valley

Visit the bridge and lanes of Dolceacqua

Head inland to Dolceacqua, a village of stone lanes gathered beneath the Doria castle. Its graceful medieval bridge over the Nervia is one of the area’s signature views, famously painted by Claude Monet. Wander through the old quarter, browse small shops and stop for a tasting of Rossese di Dolceacqua if you enjoy local wine.

Getting there: Public buses serve the valley, but connections may be limited and less frequent than trains along the coast. Check the return journey before you go; a hire car or taxi gives more flexibility.
Afternoon
Sanremo · La Pigna and seafront

Explore Sanremo’s old town and elegant promenade

Return to the coast and spend the afternoon in Sanremo. Climb into La Pigna, the old town’s maze of covered lanes, stairways and little squares, then descend towards the palm-lined Corso Imperatrice and the seafront. The contrast between the medieval quarter and the grand resort architecture is part of Sanremo’s appeal.

Worth a look: The Casino building, the Russian Orthodox Church and the harbour area. You do not need to gamble or book an attraction to enjoy the town’s architecture.

Evening
Sanremo · Ligurian food

Make dinner a celebration of local specialities

Try sardenaira, Sanremo’s savoury focaccia-like speciality topped with tomato, olives and anchovies, or choose fresh fish and pasta with pesto. If you have enjoyed the local flavours so far, browse our French Riviera food guide for the other side of the border and our Italian Riviera pizza guide for more ideas.

Day 5 · From Sanremo to Imperia: the Riviera dei Fiori

Finish further east along the Ligurian coast, with a choice of a relaxed cycle or seaside journey and time to discover Imperia’s two historic centres.

Morning
Coastal path · Ospedaletti to San Lorenzo

Enjoy a section of the Riviera dei Fiori cycle path

If you like being outdoors, rent a bicycle or take a walk on part of the former railway route that now follows the coast. The established path links Ospedaletti, Sanremo, Arma di Taggia, Riva Ligure, Santo Stefano al Mare and San Lorenzo al Mare. You do not need to cover the full distance: choose a manageable section, enjoy the sea views and stop for a coffee.

Check first: Confirm current access, bike hire, tunnel conditions and any temporary closures locally. If cycling is not for you, travel directly to Imperia by train and enjoy a longer visit there.
Afternoon
Imperia · Oneglia and Porto Maurizio

Explore Imperia’s two distinct old centres

Imperia is made up of two historic districts. Oneglia has a working harbour, lively food shops and a strong connection to the region’s olive-oil trade. Porto Maurizio rises above the sea in a tangle of lanes around the Parasio, with beautiful views and a more historic atmosphere. Give yourself time for both if you can.

Food stop: Look for Taggiasca olives, local extra-virgin olive oil, focaccia and seafood. The Museo dell’Olivo is a worthwhile extra for anyone curious about the region’s olive-growing heritage; check its opening days before visiting.

Evening
Imperia · End of the route

End with an aperitivo by the sea

Choose a waterfront table, enjoy one last Ligurian meal and take in the harbour atmosphere. From Imperia you can continue along the Italian Riviera, return west by train or use the city as a base for exploring more of Liguria.

Getting around: train, car or a combination?

The easiest option for most visitors: train along the coast

The coastal railway makes this route practical without a car. Nice, Monaco, Menton, Ventimiglia, Bordighera, Sanremo and Imperia are connected by rail, although services, journey times and cross-border connections can change. Check the current timetable for your exact travel date, especially when crossing from France into Italy. Buy the correct ticket for each operator and validate or activate it as instructed.

Useful official planning sites: SNCF Connect for French journeys and Trenitalia for Italian services.

When a car helps

A car is useful for Èze village, Dolceacqua and other inland villages, but it can be a disadvantage in the busiest coastal towns. Parking is limited or expensive in many centres, and traffic can be heavy in summer. Consider using trains for the main coastal route and arranging a taxi, bus or rental car for just the inland day.

Crossing the France–Italy border

There is no routine passport checkpoint for travel between France and Italy within the Schengen area, but carry valid identification and check entry requirements for your nationality. Allow extra flexibility for cross-border rail connections and any engineering works. The itinerary does not depend on a particular train departure.

Where to stay for five days

Option A · Two bases, less packing

Spend the first two nights in Nice and the next three nights in Sanremo or Bordighera. Nice is convenient for the French Riviera days; Sanremo has a good range of accommodation and puts you in a useful position for the Italian coast. You can travel east by train on Day 3 and keep the same Italian base for the remaining days.

Option B · Three bases, shorter daily journeys

Stay in Nice for Night 1, Menton for Night 2, and Bordighera or Sanremo for Nights 3 and 4. Finish in Imperia on Day 5. This reduces backtracking but means changing hotels more often. It works particularly well if you enjoy travelling slowly and want to experience the towns after day-trippers have left.

When to go

Late spring and early autumn are excellent for walking, gardens and village visits, with generally more comfortable temperatures than high summer. July and August are best for beach time but bring crowds, traffic and higher accommodation demand. Winter can be mild, especially on the Italian Riviera, but some attractions and seasonal services operate reduced hours.

What to pack

Bring comfortable shoes for cobbled lanes and steep village streets, a light layer for breezy evenings, sun protection and swimwear in warmer months. If you plan to cycle, check what equipment is included with the rental and bring a small water bottle.

French and Italian Riviera itinerary: FAQs

Can you visit the French and Italian Riviera in five days?

Yes, if you focus on the eastern French Riviera and western Liguria. This route covers Nice, Èze, Monaco, Menton, Ventimiglia, Bordighera, Dolceacqua, Sanremo and Imperia. It does not try to include Cannes, Saint-Tropez or the whole Italian Riviera.

Can I do this itinerary without a car?

Most coastal stops are reachable by train. The main exceptions are Èze village and Dolceacqua, where you will need to plan a bus, taxi or transfer. Check the latest timetables before committing to the order of the day.

Where should I start the trip?

Nice is the easiest starting point for this west-to-east itinerary, with an international airport and good rail connections. You can also reverse the route and begin in Imperia if that better suits your flights or onward travel.

Is one day enough for Monaco and Menton?

It is enough for a highlights visit if you start early and keep your plans selective. If you prefer museums, gardens, long lunches or a slower pace, spend an extra day in this part of the French Riviera.

What is the best base on the Italian Riviera?

Sanremo is a convenient all-round base with a broad choice of accommodation, restaurants and rail connections. Bordighera is quieter and attractive for gardens and the old town; Ventimiglia is useful for the border and inland valley trips.

Does the itinerary work in winter?

The main towns can be visited year-round, but daylight is shorter and some gardens, museums, beach services and bus connections may have reduced schedules. Check individual opening times and transport before travelling.
