30 wonderful things to do on the French Riviera

From colourful old towns and hidden coves to island escapes, hilltop villages and long Mediterranean lunches. Our independent guide to experiencing the Côte d’Azur beyond the postcard.

A locally minded guide by Inside Riviera · French Riviera, France

The French Riviera is not just a strip of glamorous beaches. It is a string of distinct towns, fishing harbours, gardens, art-filled villages and landscapes where the Alps seem to tumble into the Mediterranean. You can spend the morning wandering a centuries-old market, swim in a sheltered bay after lunch and watch the light change over the sea from a hilltop terrace. These are the experiences we would put on a Riviera wish list, whether it is your first visit or your tenth.

By the sea: the Riviera at its most beautiful

The coastline changes character as you travel west to east: grand promenades, pastel harbours, sandy stretches, rocky coves and little ports where the day still follows the rhythm of the sea.

01
Nice · Seafront and old town

Walk the Promenade des Anglais, then get lost in Vieux Nice

Start with the curve of the Baie des Anges, the palm trees and the famous blue chairs. Then head into the old town, where narrow streets lead to baroque churches, small shops, cafés and the lively Cours Saleya market. The contrast between the open seafront and the close-knit lanes is part of Nice’s charm.

Don’t miss: Climb Castle Hill (Colline du Château) for one of the city’s best views over the rooftops, port and bay.

Insider tip: Visit the old town in the morning, when the market is lively and the lanes are cooler. Check market days and hours before travelling.

Plan a visit with Explore Nice Côte d’Azur.

02
Villefranche-sur-Mer · Harbour

Take in the pastel waterfront of Villefranche-sur-Mer

Few places capture the Riviera mood so effortlessly. Colourful façades rise above a deep, sheltered bay, fishing boats bob in the harbour and the old town is made for an unhurried wander. Follow the waterfront, explore the lanes and pause for a meal overlooking the water.

Don’t miss: The old port and the covered Rue Obscure, a remarkable passage through the historic centre.

03
Antibes · Old town

Walk Antibes’ ramparts and browse its old-town market

Antibes has a wonderfully lived-in feel: a marina filled with boats, stone ramparts facing the sea, a maze of old streets and a market full of Provençal produce. It is an easy place to spend a day without following a strict itinerary.

Don’t miss: The Picasso Museum in the Château Grimaldi and the views from the ramparts. Check the museum’s current opening information before setting out.

Explore Antibes Juan-les-Pins.

04
Cannes · La Croisette and Le Suquet

See two sides of Cannes: the Croisette and Le Suquet

There is the polished Cannes of designer boutiques, grand hotels and the palm-lined Croisette, and then there is the older quarter of Le Suquet, with steep lanes, small squares and views over the old port. Make time for both; the city is more interesting when you look beyond its film-festival reputation.

Don’t miss: The view from the area around Notre-Dame d’Espérance, especially towards the harbour and the Lérins Islands.

Discover Cannes.

05
Menton · Old town and seafront

Explore Menton’s colourful old town and seaside promenade

Close to the Italian border, Menton has a softer, more relaxed atmosphere, with pastel houses climbing above the port, citrus gardens and a lovely stretch of waterfront. Take the lanes slowly: stairways, little squares and sudden sea views are the reward.

Don’t miss: The view towards the Basilica of Saint-Michel from the seafront, then a stroll around the old port.

See our Inside Riviera guide to Menton for more local ideas.

06
Saint-Tropez · Old port

Enjoy Saint-Tropez beyond the famous beach clubs

Saint-Tropez is still a working Mediterranean port beneath the glamour. Come early for the harbour, wander the old streets, visit the citadel and make time for Place des Lices. In summer, allow extra time for traffic and parking; the journey can take much longer than the map suggests.

Don’t miss: The view over the terracotta rooftops and gulf from the citadel area.

Plan with Saint-Tropez Tourisme.

07
Monaco · Principality

Spend a day discovering Monaco on foot

Although it is an independent principality rather than part of France, Monaco makes a natural Riviera day trip. Explore the old town on Le Rocher, visit the Oceanographic Museum, walk around Port Hercule and take in the architecture and gardens of Monte-Carlo.

Don’t miss: The contrast between the historic lanes around the Prince’s Palace and the grand setting of Casino Square. Check official visitor information for palace and museum access.

Discover Monaco.

08
Juan-les-Pins · Beach life

Make time for a classic beach day in Juan-les-Pins

Just west of Antibes, Juan-les-Pins is made for a slower summer day: a swim, a beachside lunch and a walk beneath the pines. It is livelier in high season, while shoulder-season visits are often more peaceful.

09
Cap d’Ail · Coastal scenery

Find a quieter corner at Plage de la Mala

Set beneath dramatic cliffs near Monaco, Mala is one of the coast’s most striking coves. The setting is beautiful, but access involves a descent and steps, and facilities can be seasonal. Check sea conditions and local access advice before you go.

Hilltop villages and the Riviera hinterland

A short journey inland can change the scenery completely: stone lanes, artist studios, perfume heritage and big views across the coast.

10
Èze · Medieval village

Climb through Èze’s medieval streets to the Exotic Garden

Perched high above the sea, Èze is one of the Riviera’s most memorable villages. Its narrow lanes wind between stone houses, galleries and small terraces before reaching the Jardin Exotique, where the views stretch across the coastline.

Don’t miss: The panorama from the top. Wear comfortable shoes: the village is steep and the paving can be uneven.

Visitor information for Èze.

11
Saint-Paul-de-Vence · Art village

Spend an afternoon among the galleries of Saint-Paul-de-Vence

Inside its medieval walls, Saint-Paul-de-Vence is full of art galleries, studios and stone passageways. It is a beautiful place to browse slowly, stop for a coffee and appreciate how the village has drawn artists and collectors for generations.

Don’t miss: The nearby Fondation Maeght, a major modern-art destination set among sculpture-filled gardens.

Discover Saint-Paul-de-Vence · Fondation Maeght.

12
Grasse · Perfume capital

Discover how perfume is made in Grasse

Grasse is the historic heart of French perfumery. Visit a perfume house or museum to learn about ingredients, extraction and composition, or book a workshop to create a fragrance of your own. It is an experience that connects the Riviera to the flowers and landscapes of its hinterland.

Don’t miss: The International Perfume Museum and the visitor experiences offered by the historic perfume houses. Check booking requirements in advance.

International Perfume Museum.

13
Biot · Craft and glass

Watch traditional glassmaking in Biot

Biot is known for its distinctive bubbled glass and artisan heritage. Explore the village’s galleries and workshops, and look for demonstrations where you can see the skill and heat involved in shaping glass by hand.

Don’t miss: The Verrerie de Biot and the village’s small artisan shops.

Verrerie de Biot visitor information.

14
Mougins · Food and art

Wander the lanes of Mougins and linger over lunch

Set above Cannes, Mougins is a handsome old village with galleries, restaurants and views towards the surrounding hills. It is an especially good choice for a leisurely lunch away from the busiest seafront streets.

15
Roquebrune-Cap-Martin · Historic village

Explore Roquebrune’s medieval village and castle

Above the coast between Monaco and Menton, the old village is a rewarding stop for narrow lanes, stone architecture and wide views. Pair it with a walk along the Cap Martin coastal path if conditions and access allow.

See our Monte Carlo destination guide for ideas nearby.

16
Gourdon · Eagle’s nest village

Take in the views from Gourdon

Further inland, Gourdon sits high above the Loup valley and offers a very different perspective on the region. Go for the village, the panorama and the feeling of being in the hills rather than on the coast. Check road and weather conditions in winter.

Art, architecture and beautiful gardens

The Riviera has inspired painters, designers, architects and gardeners. Some of its best visits are as much about the setting as the collections themselves.

17
Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat · Villa and gardens

Visit Villa Ephrussi de Rothschild

This rose-pink villa is surrounded by themed gardens on the Cap-Ferrat peninsula. It is a wonderful half-day visit for architecture, art collections and landscaped views over the sea. Allow time to explore the different garden styles rather than rushing through the house.

Check opening times and tickets.

18
Nice · Museums

Follow the artists through Nice’s museums

Nice has a remarkable cultural offer. Choose the Matisse Museum in Cimiez, the Marc Chagall National Museum or the Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (MAMAC) when open. Pair a museum visit with a walk through the gardens and historic neighbourhoods nearby.

Musée Matisse · Musée national Marc Chagall.

19
Menton · Gardens

Explore Menton’s remarkable gardens

Menton’s mild microclimate has helped create gardens filled with subtropical plants, citrus trees and extraordinary views. Choose a garden that is open during your visit and check whether advance booking or guided access is required.

Menton, Riviera & Merveilles visitor information.

20
Le Cannet · Bonnard

Discover the world of Pierre Bonnard

The Bonnard Museum in Le Cannet celebrates the painter’s connection with the region and his luminous colour palette. It is a rewarding cultural stop for anyone interested in the artists who made the south of France their subject.

Musée Bonnard.

21
La Napoule · Seaside château

Visit Château de la Napoule

On the coast west of Cannes, this château combines an unusual history, art and gardens right beside the sea. It makes a lovely change from a beach afternoon and is particularly photogenic from the waterfront.

Visitor information for Château de la Napoule.

Islands, coastal walks and outdoor adventures

Some of the Riviera’s most memorable days happen away from the main streets: on a boat, along a headland path or beneath the red rocks of the Esterel.

22
Cannes · Lérins Islands

Escape to the Lérins Islands

Take a boat from Cannes to Île Sainte-Marguerite for pine-scented paths, quiet coves and the historic Fort Royal, or visit Île Saint-Honorat for its abbey and peaceful atmosphere. Check ferry timetables and island facilities before travelling, as services vary by season and weather.

Ferries and island information.

23
Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat · Walking

Walk the coastal path around Cap-Ferrat

The peninsula’s footpaths bring you close to the water, past rocky inlets, villas and Mediterranean vegetation. Choose a section that suits your fitness and the weather; some stretches are exposed, and sea conditions can affect access.

Don’t miss: The changing views back towards Beaulieu-sur-Mer and Villefranche-sur-Mer.

24
Esterel · Red-rock coast

See the red rocks of the Corniche d’Or

Between Saint-Raphaël and Cannes, the Esterel massif meets the Mediterranean in dramatic red cliffs, small bays and winding coastal roads. Stop at signed viewpoints, explore an accessible trail or simply enjoy the contrast of red rock, green pines and blue sea.

Insider tip: Allow more time than you think for photo stops, and avoid leaving valuables visible in the car.

25
Îles d’Hyères · Porquerolles

Spend a day cycling on Porquerolles

Further west in the Var, Porquerolles is a beautiful island for walking and cycling, with pine forests, vineyards and pale-sand beaches. Hire a bicycle near the port and follow permitted routes, respecting protected areas and local rules.

Hyères visitor information and island access.

26
Coast · By boat

See the coastline from the water

A boat trip gives a different view of the Riviera’s headlands, villas and harbours. Options range from short coastal excursions to island ferries and private sailing trips. Compare departure ports, duration and what is included, and choose operators with clear safety information.

Taste the Riviera: markets, food and local rituals

A good Riviera day is not complete without time for the market, a local speciality or an outdoor table. These are experiences to enjoy slowly.

27
Provençal markets · Across the coast

Shop a traditional Provençal market

Markets are one of the easiest ways to connect with local life. Look for seasonal fruit, olives, cheeses, flowers, herbs and regional specialities. Cours Saleya in Nice is a classic, but smaller town markets can be just as rewarding. Market days and hours vary, so check locally before making a special trip.

28
Nice · Local specialities

Try the flavours of Nice

Make room for socca, a chickpea-flour pancake cooked in a hot oven, and explore other Niçoise favourites such as pissaladière, petits farcis and salade niçoise. Try them in a traditional restaurant or market stall and enjoy the food as part of the city’s identity.

29
Vineyards · Bellet and Var

Taste local wines and discover Riviera vineyards

The region has wine-growing areas close to the coast and inland. Bellet, in the hills above Nice, is a distinctive appellation; further west, the Var is known for rosé as well as other styles. Book tastings directly with producers and arrange transport if you plan to drink.

30
Evenings · All along the coast

Make time for an evening passeggiata-style stroll

As the heat softens, join the gentle evening rhythm: a walk along the seafront, a circuit around the harbour, an ice cream in the old town or an aperitif on a terrace. Every town has its own version, and often the simplest plan becomes the highlight of the day.

How to plan a French Riviera trip

Where should you start?

For a first visit: Nice is a practical base with an airport, a wide choice of accommodation and rail connections along much of the coast.

For old-town charm: Antibes and Menton offer very different atmospheres and make good bases for exploring nearby places.

For glamour and events: Cannes, Monaco and Saint-Tropez each have a distinct identity, but can be busy and expensive at peak times.

When is the best time to visit?

Late spring and early autumn are often appealing for walking, sightseeing and outdoor dining, with milder temperatures than high summer. July and August bring the busiest beach season and heavier traffic. Winter is quieter, and the coast remains attractive for markets, museums, gardens and seasonal events, although some attractions and boat services operate reduced schedules.

Do you need a car?

Not necessarily. The coastal train is useful for many major towns between Cannes, Antibes, Nice, Monaco and Menton. Buses serve some hill villages and inland areas, while a car can make remote viewpoints and parts of the Var easier to reach. For Saint-Tropez in summer, check traffic and boat or public-transport alternatives before setting off.

How many days do you need?

Three days can give you a taste of Nice and a couple of nearby stops. Five to seven days allows a more relaxed mix of eastern and western Riviera, while a longer stay gives you time for the villages, islands and inland countryside without rushing.

French Riviera: frequently asked questions

What are the best things to do on the French Riviera?

Highlights include exploring Nice’s old town, visiting Antibes and Cannes, discovering hilltop Èze and Saint-Paul-de-Vence, walking the Cap-Ferrat coastal path, visiting the Lérins Islands and enjoying Menton’s gardens and old town. The best mix depends on whether you prefer beaches, culture, food, walking or village life.

Which towns should first-time visitors see?

Nice is an excellent starting point. Villefranche-sur-Mer, Antibes, Èze, Menton and Cannes each offer a different side of the coast. Monaco is a nearby independent principality and an easy addition to many itineraries.

Can you explore the French Riviera without a car?

Yes. Trains connect many of the main coastal towns, and local buses reach a number of inland destinations. Some hill villages and remote natural areas are less straightforward, so check routes and timetables before you travel.

Is the French Riviera worth visiting outside summer?

Yes. Spring and autumn are excellent for sightseeing and walking, while winter offers a quieter atmosphere, local markets, gardens and seasonal celebrations. Check opening times because some attractions and boat routes are seasonal.

Is Monaco part of the French Riviera?

Monaco is a sovereign city-state, not part of France. It is geographically on the French Riviera and is commonly included in Côte d’Azur travel itineraries, so we include it here as a nearby destination.
