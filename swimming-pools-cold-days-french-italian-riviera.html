Where to Swim on a Cold Day on the Riviera

Indoor heated pools, warm spa water and winter swimming spots from Menton and Monaco to Bordighera, Sanremo and Imperia.

An independent Inside Riviera guide · November 2026

A chilly mistral, a grey afternoon or a rainy winter weekend does not have to mean giving up on a swim. Along the French and Italian Riviera, you can find proper indoor lap pools, heated spa pools and a few year-round hotel facilities. The trick is knowing the difference: a public swimming pool is usually the best-value option for lengths, while a spa or hotel pool is more about warm water, hydrotherapy and a relaxing day out.

Our quick picks

  • Best value in Monaco: Centre Nautique Prince Albert II, the indoor 50-metre municipal pool.
  • Best warm-water spa: Thermes Marins Monte-Carlo, with an indoor heated seawater pool and wellness circuit.
  • Best public pool around Cannes: Piscine Montfleury, with a 28°C main pool and 30°C spa pool in winter.
  • Best indoor option near Bordighera: the municipal pool at Palasport Emilio Biancheri.
  • Best resort-style option in western Liguria: Villa Giada in Imperia, whose covered pool is heated year-round; check whether day access is available.

Monaco and the eastern Riviera

Monaco · Public indoor swimming

1. Centre Nautique Prince Albert II – Stade Louis II

If you want to swim proper lengths rather than spend the afternoon in a spa, this is the most straightforward winter choice in Monaco. The municipal indoor complex at Stade Louis II has a 50-metre Olympic pool, a learners' pool and a diving area. It operates through the colder months, with a short summer maintenance closure. Public access, lane availability and session times can vary, so check the municipality's sports timetable before setting off.

Monaco · Warm indoor seawater

2. Thermes Marins Monte-Carlo

For a special occasion or a slow wellness day, the Thermes Marins Monte-Carlo offers a heated indoor seawater pool overlooking the port, alongside sauna, hammam, whirlpool and fitness facilities. It is a premium spa rather than a municipal pool, and day access is subject to the spa's current conditions and reservation rules. Ask exactly what the wellness pass includes before booking; treatments may be extra.

Best for: adults looking for a warm-water spa experience, not budget lane swimming.

Monaco · Small public indoor pool

3. Piscine Saint-Charles

This compact municipal pool in Monte-Carlo is another practical option for a swim when the weather is cold. It is an indoor neighbourhood pool rather than a resort facility, with a shorter basin than the Olympic pool at Stade Louis II. Check the current public-swim timetable, entry rules and any seasonal maintenance closures with Monaco's sports service.

Menton · Seasonal public swimming

4. Check the local municipal pool timetable

Menton and the surrounding communes have public sports facilities, but timetables and access can change with school lessons, clubs and maintenance. Before travelling, contact the local pool or the Menton, Riviera & Merveilles Tourist Office for the current public-swimming schedule. Do not assume an outdoor summer pool is heated or open in winter.

Nice, Antibes and Cannes

Cannes · Public heated pool

5. Piscine Montfleury

One of the strongest all-round choices for a cold day in Cannes is Piscine Montfleury. The main 25-metre pool is heated to around 28°C, while the spa pool with massage jets and geysers is listed at around 30°C in winter. There is also a paddling pool for younger children. It is a municipal facility, so it is much more practical for regular swimming than a luxury hotel spa. Check the city timetable for school-holiday changes and maintenance closures.

Best for: families, casual swimmers and people who want a warmer leisure pool as well as lanes.

Cannes · Public aquatic centre

6. Grand Bleu Aquatic Centre

The Grand Bleu has a 50-metre pool and a second pool with massage jets and air bubblers, both listed as heated to 28°C. It is a large public sports facility with swimming-club and general-public use. The pools are outdoors, however, so even heated water may not feel like a cosy indoor swim on a cold, windy day. Choose it when you want to train or swim laps and the weather is merely cool rather than miserable.

Cannes · Hotel spa

7. Eden Hôtel & Spa

The Eden Spa advertises an indoor heated pool at approximately 30°C, with hydrojet features, sauna and steam room. This is a hotel spa rather than a public swimming pool: hotel residents have access, and outside visitors should enquire directly about a spa booking or day access. Its outdoor rooftop pool is seasonal and is not the winter option.

Antibes · Thalassotherapy

8. Thalazur Antibes – check its current operating status

Thalazur Antibes has been known for its indoor heated seawater pools and marine wellness area, with hydrotherapy features, sauna and hammam. However, its operating status and access arrangements have been subject to change. Treat this as a venue to verify directly before making plans, rather than assuming the pools are currently open to day visitors. Ask about the indoor pool, public day access and children's hours specifically.

Nice · Hotel indoor pools

9. Look for a hotel spa day in Nice

Nice has several hotels with indoor heated pools, including high-end properties such as the Boscolo and the Hôtel du Couvent. These are attractive winter options, but hotel pool access is generally reserved for guests or spa clients and should never be assumed to include a standalone swim. Ask about a day pass, minimum spend, age restrictions and whether pool access is included with a treatment.

The Italian Riviera: Bordighera, Sanremo and Imperia

Bordighera · Public indoor swimming

10. Palasport Emilio Biancheri – Piscina Comunale

This is one of the most useful public indoor pool options in the western Riviera. The Palasport Emilio Biancheri has a 25-metre, eight-lane main pool and a smaller teaching pool. It welcomes visitors as well as local clubs, with single-entry public swimming listed. The published visitor information includes weekday, Saturday and Sunday sessions, but always check the latest timetable before travelling. Bring a swimming cap, flip-flops and towel; the facility uses a paid key system for showers and hairdryers.

Best for: lane swimming, families and visitors staying around Bordighera or Vallecrosia.

Sanremo · Public swimming

11. Centro Natatorio Comunale di Sanremo

Sanremo's municipal swimming centre is listed as an all-year facility, with lane swimming, aquagym and courses. It is the first place to check if you are based in Sanremo and want an ordinary swim rather than a spa day. Public timetables may be divided around lessons and club training, so look for the current nuoto libero sessions before you go.

Imperia · Resort indoor pool

12. Villa Giada Resort – Piscina Azzurra

For a warmer, more relaxed swim, Villa Giada above Imperia advertises its covered Piscina Azzurra as heated throughout the year, with water starting at around 24°C. This is a resort facility, not a municipal pool, and access may be tied to accommodation or a wellness package. Contact the resort to ask whether non-residents can book a pool or spa day, what the water temperature is on your date, and whether children are admitted.

Best for: a relaxed winter break or a family stay. Note that 24°C is heated, but may still feel cool if you are expecting a hot spa pool.

Imperia · Public indoor swimming

13. Piscina Felice Cascione

The municipal Piscina Felice Cascione is Imperia's main public swimming facility. In September 2026 the city announced a management transition and a planned October reopening, with public swimming and competitive activities expected to resume. Because this is a recent operational change, confirm that public sessions are running and check the current timetable before setting out.

Diano Marina and nearby · Hotel pools

14. Ask about indoor hotel pools before booking

Along the coast between Imperia and Diano Marina, some hotels and resorts have pools or wellness areas, but many are outdoor and seasonal. If you are looking for a cold-weather swim, ask specifically whether the pool is indoors, covered, heated and open to non-residents. A listing that simply says “pool” often refers to a summer garden pool.

Which kind of pool should you choose?

For swimming lengths and fitness

Choose a municipal pool such as Monaco's Centre Nautique Prince Albert II, Cannes Montfleury, Bordighera's Palasport or the public facilities in Sanremo and Imperia. These are designed for swimming, lessons and training. Check lane availability and public-swim slots.

For warm water and relaxation

Choose a spa or thalassotherapy centre such as Thermes Marins Monte-Carlo or a hotel spa in Cannes. Expect higher prices, possible booking requirements and restrictions on children's access. Confirm that pool access is included in the pass you purchase.

For a family swim

Look for a leisure pool with a shallow or teaching basin, such as Montfleury in Cannes or the smaller pool at Bordighera's Palasport. Ask about age limits, adult supervision, swim caps and family sessions.

For a hotel-style pool day

Ask a resort such as Villa Giada whether it sells day access. Do not assume that a hotel with a beautiful pool will admit non-guests, particularly during winter when indoor facilities may be reserved for residents or spa bookings.

What to know before you go

  • Indoor is not the same as heated. Check both the pool's location and its published water temperature.
  • Covered pools vary. A retractable roof or open-sided enclosure can feel very different from a fully indoor pool when the air is cold.
  • Check public-swim hours. Municipal pools often reserve lanes for schools, clubs and lessons.
  • Ask about day access. Hotel and spa pools may be guests-only, require a treatment booking or have limited non-resident slots.
  • Check family rules. Some wellness pools restrict children to certain hours or do not admit young children at all.
  • Pack the right kit. A swimming cap is compulsory at many Italian and French public pools. Bring flip-flops, towel and suitable swimwear; some venues prohibit loose board shorts.
  • Confirm maintenance closures. Annual draining, school holidays and private events can affect opening times.
Our tip: If you simply want to swim on a cold day without spending a fortune, start with a municipal indoor pool. Save the spa pools for when you want to make the swim part of a longer wellness day.
