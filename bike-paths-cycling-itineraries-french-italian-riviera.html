The Riviera by Bike

The prettiest coastal cycle paths, relaxed half-day rides and full-day itineraries, with lovely places to stop for coffee, focaccia, lunch and an ice cream.

There is something special about discovering the Riviera on two wheels. The sea is never far away, old railway lines have become beautiful coastal paths, and fishing villages, harbours and beach cafés make natural places to pause. You can enjoy a gentle spin along the Promenade des Anglais, follow the old railway route through the Riviera dei Fiori in Liguria, or link Cannes, Antibes and Nice on a longer Mediterranean ride.

This guide focuses on routes that work for a holiday: where to ride, how far to go, where the surface is easiest, and where to stop for a good coffee or a leisurely lunch. Distances are approximate and can vary with your chosen start point and detours. Always check local route notices before setting off, especially where cycle paths meet shared roads or busy promenades.

The best bike paths on the French Riviera

Easy · Family-friendly · Nice

La Littorale: Nice and the Baie des Anges

Nice is one of the easiest places to start cycling on the French Riviera. The La Littorale route follows the curve of the Baie des Anges, taking in the Promenade des Anglais and continuing west towards Saint-Laurent-du-Var and the Cagnes-sur-Mer seafront. The Nice Côte d'Azur Tourist Office describes a 15-kilometre ride along the bay, or around 30 kilometres there and back. It is a lovely choice for a relaxed ride, with the sea beside you for much of the way.

The route is mostly flat, but it is not a closed cycling circuit: promenades can be busy with walkers, runners and families, especially in summer. Slow down around pedestrian areas and give people plenty of room.

Easy · Cannes and Golfe-Juan

La Croisette to Golfe-Juan

Cannes has several pleasant seafront cycling sections. From the old port, ride along La Croisette towards Palm Beach, then continue around Pointe Croisette and east towards Golfe-Juan. Cannes Tourism describes the Cannes–Golfe-Juan ride as approximately 12 kilometres return and suitable for all levels. You get palm-lined promenades, views towards the Lérins Islands and the beaches of Mouré Rouge.

There are cycle tracks as well as shared sections, so follow the local signs rather than assuming the entire route is separated from traffic. The stretch is ideal for a short outing with a café stop at Port Canto or a longer pause in Golfe-Juan.

Easy · Cannes and Mandelieu

Cannes to Mandelieu-la-Napoule

Head west from central Cannes along the coast towards La Bocca and Mandelieu-la-Napoule. Cannes Tourism describes a roughly 20-kilometre return ride to Mandelieu, with the option to add more distance towards the red rocks of the Estérel and the Corniche d'Or. The seafront scenery is beautiful, especially where the blue water contrasts with the red Estérel hills.

For a relaxed holiday ride, keep to the coastal section and turn around in Mandelieu. The extra Corniche d'Or roads are a different proposition: they are more demanding and include road riding, so they are better for confident cyclists who have checked the route.

Long-distance · EuroVelo 8

Cannes, Antibes, Cagnes-sur-Mer and Nice

The Cannes–Nice section of the Mediterranean Cycle Route (EuroVelo 8) is one of the most rewarding ways to see this stretch of coast. The official route is about 36 kilometres one way. It follows the waterfront from Cannes, continues through Juan-les-Pins and Antibes, passes Villeneuve-Loubet marina and joins the cycle track through Cagnes-sur-Mer before reaching Nice.

Although it is graded as a beginner stage by France Vélo Tourisme, the route is a mixture of dedicated cycle tracks, shared roads and urban links. It is not one uninterrupted protected cycle lane. Allow time for traffic lights, busy promenade areas and sightseeing stops. The covered market and harbour in Antibes are especially tempting places to pause.

Official Cannes–Nice EuroVelo 8 stage · View the coastal route

A note about Nice to Menton: EuroVelo 8 continues east from Nice towards Menton, but this is not a flat seaside promenade. The official route includes a climb towards Èze Pass at about 507 metres, with descents towards La Turbie, Roquebrune-Cap-Martin and Menton. It is a scenic, challenging ride for fit cyclists, not the best choice for a casual family outing.

The best bike path on the Italian Riviera

Easy · Mostly flat · Liguria

Riviera dei Fiori Coastal Cycle Path

The star of western Liguria is the Pista Ciclabile del Parco Costiero della Riviera dei Fiori. Built along the former coastal railway, it links seaside towns and beaches on a largely flat, paved route, with tunnels, old station buildings, sea views and plenty of places to stop. The best-known central stretch runs between Ospedaletti, Sanremo, Arma di Taggia, Riva Ligure, Santo Stefano al Mare and San Lorenzo al Mare.

Italy's official tourism portal highlights the 24-kilometre stretch from San Lorenzo al Mare to Ospedaletti. The route has also been extended eastwards in stages; local route information reports a further connection towards Andora opened in June 2026. Because the network and access points have been changing, check the latest local map before planning a longer point-to-point ride.

The path is shared by cyclists and pedestrians, with marked lanes in many sections. It is ideal for relaxed cycling, families and e-bikes, but slow down in tunnels, at crossings and near busy beach entrances.

Official Italia.it route guide · Riviera dei Fiori DMO route information · Find the cycle path

Important if you are staying in Ventimiglia or Bordighera: the dedicated Riviera dei Fiori coastal path does not run west from Ospedaletti all the way to Bordighera or Ventimiglia. Do not assume there is a continuous traffic-free cycle path along that stretch. The Bicknell Cycle Route is a separate, much longer and more demanding mountain/gravel itinerary, not a flat seafront alternative.

French Riviera: half-day cycling itineraries

Half day · Easy · About 15–30 km

Ride 1: Nice to Cagnes-sur-Mer and back

Start: Promenade des Anglais, Nice. Ride: follow La Littorale west along the Baie des Anges towards Saint-Laurent-du-Var and Cagnes-sur-Mer, then return the same way. The official Nice route is around 15 kilometres one way, so the full return ride is about 30 kilometres. You can make it shorter by turning around at the airport area or Saint-Laurent-du-Var.

  1. Start with coffee in Nice: pause near the seafront before setting off. Café du Cycliste by Port Lympia is a well-known cycling-themed café, while Le Paradis du Fruit on the Promenade des Anglais is an easy breakfast or juice stop.
  2. Ride west along the bay: enjoy the sea views and the airport approach, keeping your speed down on shared sections.
  3. Brunch or lunch in Cagnes: Copenhagen Coffee Lab at 28 Promenade de la Plage is a good coffee-and-pastry stop. For a longer lunch, Le Petit Niçois Bio at 99 Promenade de la Plage is a seafront restaurant.
  4. Return to Nice: follow the same route, or shorten the ride if you have had a long lunch.

Half day · Very easy · About 12 km return

Ride 2: Cannes to Golfe-Juan

Start: the old port in Cannes. Ride: follow La Croisette east to Palm Beach and continue around Pointe Croisette towards Golfe-Juan. Cannes Tourism gives the return distance as about 12 kilometres, making this one of the easiest coastal rides in the area.

  1. Coffee before departure: Cafés Sauvage in central Cannes is a specialty-coffee and brunch option. If you prefer to start beside the sea, Port Canto has waterfront cafés and restaurants.
  2. Pause at Palm Beach: enjoy views across the water towards the Lérins Islands and the beaches around Pointe Croisette.
  3. Continue to Golfe-Juan: stop for a drink or a light lunch before retracing your route to Cannes.
  4. Finish on the Croisette: leave time for a stroll through central Cannes or a swim at the beach.

French Riviera: full-day cycling itinerary

Full day · 36 km one way · Moderate day out

Ride 3: Cannes to Nice via Antibes and Cagnes-sur-Mer

This is a lovely full-day point-to-point ride for anyone who wants to see several Riviera towns in one go. The official EuroVelo 8 stage is around 36 kilometres from Cannes to Nice. It is mostly coastal, but includes urban links and shared-road sections, so it is best for riders who are comfortable cycling in town traffic.

  1. Breakfast in Cannes: start with coffee and something to eat at Cafés Sauvage or another café near the old port.
  2. Juan-les-Pins: follow the coast east and take a short break around the beaches and pine-shaded waterfront.
  3. Antibes: allow time for the old town, the covered market and Port Vauban. This is a good place for an early lunch or a picnic stop.
  4. Villeneuve-Loubet: continue past the marina and follow the signed route towards Cagnes-sur-Mer.
  5. Cagnes-sur-Mer: take a proper lunch break on the Promenade de la Plage. Le Petit Niçois Bio, Cararocca and Copenhagen Coffee Lab are options along the seafront, depending on whether you want a full meal or just coffee and a snack.
  6. Finish in Nice: ride the final coastal stretch to the Promenade des Anglais. Celebrate with an ice cream or a drink near the harbour.

Getting back: this is best planned as a one-way ride. You may be able to return by TER train, but check the current service, station access and bicycle rules before relying on it. Bike spaces can be limited, especially at busy times. Alternatively, arrange a second vehicle or ride back if you have the energy.

Italian Riviera: half-day cycling itinerary

Half day · Easy · Around 12–20 km

Ride 4: Sanremo, Bussana and Arma di Taggia

Start: Sanremo's seafront cycle path. This is an easy way to enjoy the best-known section of the Riviera dei Fiori route without committing to the full distance. Ride east from Sanremo towards Bussana a Mare and Arma di Taggia, then return along the same flat coastal path. The exact distance depends on where you start and how far you continue.

  1. Begin in Sanremo: have an espresso near the old port or the seafront before joining the cycle path.
  2. Ride towards Tre Ponti and Bussana a Mare: the route passes beaches and old railway infrastructure, with plenty of places to stop for photographs.
  3. Snack at Il Chiosco di Zio Totò: this casual kiosk is listed directly on the cycle path around km 13, in the Bussana a Mare area. It is a handy stop for a quick bite or cold drink.
  4. Another option: Paninoteca La Cueva is in the Tre Ponti area, beside the path, for a sandwich or informal break.
  5. Return to Sanremo: finish with a gelato or a relaxed lunch in town.

Half day · Easy · Around 15–25 km

Ride 5: Sanremo to Ospedaletti and back

For a shorter ride heading west, follow the cycle path from Sanremo towards Ospedaletti. The route passes the old railway tunnels and coastal viewpoints, with the Capo Nero tunnel one of the memorable features. It is a straightforward out-and-back ride, but bring a light layer: tunnels can feel cooler than the sunny promenade.

  1. Start by the Sanremo harbour: Bar Portovecchio (Il Baretto) is right beside the cycle route and makes an easy coffee stop.
  2. Ride west towards La Vesca: this café-restaurant sits directly on the path and is a natural place to pause for a drink, lunch or an aperitivo by the sea.
  3. Continue to Ospedaletti: enjoy the seafront and turn around when you are ready.
  4. Back in Sanremo: head into the centre for a longer lunch or dinner. Marzio Bistrot is one option in the old town; check its service times before arriving.

Italian Riviera: full-day cycling itinerary

Full day · Easy to moderate · About 24 km one way

Ride 6: Ospedaletti to San Lorenzo al Mare

This is the classic full-day Riviera dei Fiori ride. The main 24-kilometre stretch between Ospedaletti and San Lorenzo al Mare follows the former railway line along the coast. It is mostly flat and paved, making it suitable for ordinary bikes and e-bikes. Ride one way and arrange transport back, or turn it into a longer return day of roughly 48 kilometres with plenty of café stops.

  1. Start in Ospedaletti: join the cycle path by the former station and head east through the Capo Nero tunnel towards Sanremo.
  2. Sanremo: take a break near the harbour or continue into town for a coffee and a look at the old centre.
  3. La Vesca: stop at this cycle-path café-restaurant for a drink or lunch with a sea view.
  4. Bussana a Mare and Arma di Taggia: continue east, with optional snack stops at Il Chiosco di Zio Totò or La Cueva in the Tre Ponti/Bussana area.
  5. Riva Ligure and Santo Stefano al Mare: pass through these seaside towns, ideal for a relaxed coffee or gelato break.
  6. San Lorenzo al Mare: finish at the western end of the classic 24-kilometre section. Enjoy lunch or a final drink by the marina before returning or arranging a lift.

Optional extension: the cycle network has been extended in stages east of San Lorenzo al Mare, towards Imperia and Diano Marina/Andora. Recent local information reports a continuous route as far as Andora, but check the latest signage and access before setting out on the extension.

Longer full day · Flexible distance

Ride 7: Imperia and the Riviera dei Fiori path

If you are based in Imperia, start at the Parco Urbano San Leonardo and join the coastal route. Explore the seafront, then ride west towards Santo Stefano al Mare and Sanremo for a longer day, or keep it shorter and explore Imperia's two historic centres, Oneglia and Porto Maurizio. The route is a lovely way to link the marina, beaches and old town, but check the current path connections and your chosen turnaround point.

  1. Coffee in Oneglia: start near the harbour, where Caffè del Porto is a handy waterfront stop.
  2. Ride the seafront: pass the Parco Urbano, where Parco Urbano Cafè is a relaxed place for a drink or snack.
  3. Continue west: follow the signed coastal path towards Santo Stefano al Mare if you want a longer ride.
  4. Return to Imperia: finish with a gelato around Oneglia harbour or explore Porto Maurizio and the Parasio old quarter on foot.

Bike hire, equipment and practical tips

  • Choose the right bike. A city bike or hybrid is perfect for the flat Riviera dei Fiori path and the Nice seafront. A gravel bike or road bike is more suitable for longer routes with shared roads or climbs. An e-bike makes the hills around Nice–Menton and the Ligurian hinterland more manageable.
  • Hire locally. Look for rental shops in Nice, Cannes, Antibes, Sanremo and Imperia. Ask whether the rental includes a helmet, lock, lights, repair kit and roadside support. Reserve e-bikes in advance in high season.
  • Check train rules before planning a one-way trip. TER services can be useful for returning from a ride, but bike capacity and rules vary by train and time. Check the operator's current conditions before relying on rail transport.
  • Start early in summer. The coast can be very hot, and the promenades become crowded later in the day. Carry water and take breaks in the shade.
  • Be considerate on shared paths. Both countries have busy seafront promenades used by pedestrians, children, runners and cyclists. Slow down, use your bell politely and give walkers space.
  • Lights for tunnels. Some sections of the Ligurian route pass through former railway tunnels. Use working front and rear lights and avoid stopping where visibility is limited.
  • Check wind and weather. Coastal wind can make an easy return ride harder. In the hills, weather and road conditions can change quickly.
  • Plan café stops, but check opening days. Small bars and kiosks may have seasonal hours, and restaurants can close between lunch and dinner. Check directly before setting off, particularly outside summer.
  • Secure your bike. Use a proper lock when stopping for lunch or sightseeing, and do not leave valuables in bike bags.
Our favourite easy rides: La Littorale from Nice towards Cagnes-sur-Mer is the best French Riviera choice for a simple family-friendly outing. In Italy, the Riviera dei Fiori path between Sanremo, Arma di Taggia and San Lorenzo al Mare is hard to beat for a relaxed day beside the sea, with cafés and beach stops all along the way.

Make the ride part of the holiday

The best Riviera cycling days are not necessarily about distance. They are about rolling beside the Mediterranean, stopping for a cappuccino, browsing a market, taking a swim and lingering over lunch. Keep the route flexible, choose a comfortable pace and leave time to enjoy the towns along the way.

Route lengths, path connections and café opening hours can change.
