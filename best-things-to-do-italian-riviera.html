30 wonderful things to do on the Italian Riviera

From Ventimiglia and Bordighera to Sanremo and Imperia: discover seaside promenades, medieval villages, botanical gardens, local markets and the quieter corners of Liguria’s Riviera dei Fiori.

An independent local guide by Inside Riviera · Western Liguria, Italy

The western Italian Riviera is a coast of contrasts. Along the water, Belle Époque promenades, fishing harbours and sandy beaches sit beside palm gardens and small seaside towns. A short drive inland, the landscape changes into olive groves, stone villages and steep green valleys. This guide focuses on the stretch between Ventimiglia and Imperia: a compact part of Liguria with plenty to explore, whether you are here for a weekend or live nearby and want fresh ideas for a day out.

Ventimiglia and the borderlands

Start at the western edge of Italy, where the coast meets France and the Nervia and Roya valleys lead towards the Ligurian hills.

01
OLD TOWN

Explore Ventimiglia Alta

Leave the modern waterfront and climb into Ventimiglia Alta, where steep lanes, stone stairways and quiet squares gather around the cathedral and the Romanesque baptistery. It is one of the most atmospheric historic centres on this stretch of coast, and the views open up as you climb.

Local tip: Go in the morning or late afternoon, when the lanes are cooler and the light catches the old façades.

02
LOCAL LIFE

Browse Ventimiglia’s Friday market

The weekly market is part of the town’s identity, drawing shoppers from across the border for clothes, household finds, food and everyday Italian life. It is lively rather than polished, and a good way to experience the border town beyond its beaches.

Local tip: Market arrangements can change on public holidays; check locally before making a special trip.

Plan your visit ↗

03
GARDENS

Visit the Hanbury Botanical Gardens

At La Mortola, just west of Ventimiglia, the Hanbury Gardens spill down a coastal slope towards the sea. Created by the Hanbury family in the nineteenth century, the garden brings together plants from around the world, historic structures and beautiful Mediterranean views.

Local tip: Allow at least a couple of hours and wear comfortable shoes: paths and terraces run up and down the hillside. Check seasonal opening times before visiting.

Plan your visit ↗

04
HISTORY

Discover the Balzi Rossi prehistoric site

Near the French border, the Balzi Rossi caves and museum offer a fascinating change from the usual seaside itinerary. The caves preserve evidence of prehistoric human life, making this one of the area's most distinctive cultural visits.

Local tip: Pair it with a coastal walk around Grimaldi, but check museum opening days and access in advance.

Plan your visit ↗

05
NATURE

Walk around the Nervia river mouth

The Nervia river mouth near Camporosso and Ventimiglia is a quieter natural corner, with wetland habitats and open views. It is a reminder that this coast is not only beaches and marinas: river landscapes and birdlife sit close to the built-up shoreline.

Local tip: Bring binoculars if you enjoy birdwatching, and keep to public paths and protected areas.

Bordighera, Vallecrosia and the inland villages

This is one of the most varied parts of the Riviera dei Fiori: gardens and elegant seaside streets on the coast, with memorable stone villages just inland.

06
OLD TOWN

Wander through Bordighera Alta

Bordighera’s upper town is a lovely tangle of narrow lanes, arches, small squares and stone houses. Take your time rather than following a checklist: the pleasure is in the details, the glimpses of the sea and the sudden little piazzas.

Local tip: Combine the old town with a stroll down towards the seafront and the palm-lined promenade.

Plan your visit ↗

07
SEASIDE

Follow Bordighera’s seafront promenade

Bordighera has a graceful, old-fashioned seaside atmosphere. Its waterfront is made for an unhurried walk, with palms, beach clubs, cafés and views along the coast. It is particularly pleasant outside the busiest summer hours.

Local tip: Sunset is a lovely time for a walk; in summer, expect beach areas to be busier and more seasonal.

Plan your visit ↗

08
GARDENS

Visit the Pallanca Exotic Garden

Above the sea in Bordighera, the Pallanca Exotic Garden is a remarkable collection of cacti and succulents arranged on a steep rocky site. It feels very different from a conventional flower garden and makes an unusual stop for plant lovers.

Local tip: The terrain is steep in places. Check opening days and accessibility before setting out.

Plan your visit ↗

09
CULTURE

Step into Bordighera’s artistic past

Bordighera attracted artists, writers and visitors from northern Europe, and that history is still part of its character. Look out for the Clarence Bicknell Museum and the town’s historic villas and gardens; they tell the story of a resort that grew around culture as much as the sea.

Local tip: Check the museum’s current opening schedule, as smaller cultural venues may have limited hours.

Plan your visit ↗

10
BEACH

Take a slow beach day in Vallecrosia

Vallecrosia is a practical choice for a relaxed day beside the water, with a long seafront and a more everyday feel than some of the Riviera’s best-known resorts. It is easy to combine with Bordighera, just along the coast.

Local tip: Beach services vary by season. In summer, check whether you prefer a public stretch or a managed beach club.

Plan your visit ↗

11
HILLTOP VILLAGE

Discover Dolceacqua and its old bridge

In the Nervia valley inland from the coast, Dolceacqua is one of the area’s most memorable villages. Its arched medieval bridge leads towards a maze of lanes beneath the Doria castle, with stone houses and little places to stop for a glass of local wine.

Local tip: Visit earlier in the day or stay for lunch; parking is generally outside the tight historic core.

Plan your visit ↗

12
HILLTOP VILLAGE

Spend an afternoon in Apricale

Apricale climbs a hillside in a series of narrow stone lanes, stairways and small squares. Artworks and details appear around corners, while the views reach across the surrounding green hills. It is an ideal place to slow down after time on the coast.

Local tip: Wear shoes with grip: the lanes are steep, stepped and sometimes polished smooth.

Plan your visit ↗

13
OUTDOORS

Cool off in the Barbaira valley at Rocchetta Nervina

Rocchetta Nervina is known for its stone lanes, old bridges and the clear pools of the Barbaira stream. The valley is especially tempting in warm weather, but the water and terrain are natural rather than managed bathing facilities.

Local tip: Conditions can change quickly after rain. Check local guidance, avoid risky jumps and never enter the stream during or after storms.

Plan your visit ↗

14
HILLTOP VILLAGE

Explore Pigna’s medieval lanes

Further up the Nervia valley, Pigna is a beautiful stone village of covered passages, arches and winding lanes. Its setting feels distinctly inland and mountainous, even though the sea is not far away.

Local tip: Allow time to explore on foot; the best details are tucked into the smaller lanes.

Plan your visit ↗

15
HILLTOP VILLAGE

Visit Seborga for its unusual story and views

Perched in the hills above Bordighera, Seborga is known for its self-styled principality and its distinctive local traditions. Whatever you make of the story, the village itself is a pleasant place for a wander, with stone streets and wide views towards the coast.

Local tip: Treat the principality identity as local history and folklore, not a recognised independent state.

Plan your visit ↗

16
VIEWS & VILLAGES

Look at the coast from Perinaldo

Perinaldo is a peaceful hill village associated with astronomer Giovanni Domenico Cassini. Its elevated position brings wide views over the valleys and towards the sea, and its observatory heritage adds an unexpected layer to a village visit.

Local tip: A car is useful for reaching many of the inland villages; check access and opening times for local cultural sites.

Ospedaletti and Sanremo

The Riviera becomes more urban and grand here, with promenades, historic resort architecture, gardens and the maze-like old quarter of Sanremo.

17
SEASIDE

Enjoy the quieter seaside mood of Ospedaletti

Ospedaletti sits in a sheltered curve of coast between Capo Nero and Capo Sant’Ampelio. It is a good stop for a seaside coffee, a swim in season or a gentle walk, especially if you want a calmer alternative to central Sanremo.

Local tip: The town is also a useful access point for the western end of the Riviera dei Fiori coastal path.

Plan your visit ↗

18
ARCHITECTURE

Walk Sanremo’s elegant Corso Imperatrice

Sanremo’s seafront promenade is lined with palms and grand buildings, and has a distinctly Belle Époque feel. The Russian Orthodox Church, with its colourful domes, is one of the best-known landmarks along this stretch.

Local tip: Make time to look inland too: Sanremo’s character changes quickly between the waterfront and the old town.

Plan your visit ↗

19
OLD TOWN

Get lost in La Pigna, Sanremo’s old town

La Pigna rises above the modern centre in a maze of covered lanes, staircases, arches and little squares. It is one of the most rewarding places to explore slowly in Sanremo, with viewpoints and glimpses of everyday neighbourhood life.

Local tip: The climb can be steep. Start near the lower lanes and work your way upwards at your own pace.

Plan your visit ↗

20
CULTURE

See Sanremo’s Casino and historic centre

The Casino di Sanremo is one of the town’s signature buildings and a reminder of its grand resort era. Even if you do not go inside, the architecture and surrounding streets are worth seeing as part of a walk through the centre.

Local tip: Access to gaming rooms and events has its own rules; check the official site if you plan to enter.

Plan your visit ↗

21
GARDENS

Visit Villa Ormond and its gardens

Villa Ormond offers a green pause from the busy centre, with mature trees, landscaped grounds and a peaceful atmosphere. It is a good place to slow down between Sanremo’s old town, seafront and cultural sights.

Local tip: Check access arrangements for the villa itself; the gardens and any events may have different schedules.

Plan your visit ↗

22
ARTS & VILLAGES

Make time for Bussana Vecchia

Bussana Vecchia is an unusual artists’ village in the hills above Sanremo. After the 1887 earthquake, the old settlement was abandoned and later became home to artists and craftspeople. Today, its lanes, studios and atmospheric ruins make it a memorable detour.

Local tip: It is a lived-in village, not a theme park. Respect private homes and workshops, and check which studios are open.

Plan your visit ↗

Taggia, Arma and the coastal path

From an atmospheric inland historic centre to long sandy beaches and a former railway line reclaimed for walking and cycling, this stretch is made for easy-going days outdoors.

23
HISTORY

Explore Taggia’s medieval centre

A short way inland from Arma di Taggia, Taggia has a handsome historic centre with stone bridges, arcaded lanes and an important religious heritage. It is also closely associated with Taggiasca olives, one of the area's best-known local products.

Local tip: Combine the village with a visit to an olive-oil producer or a meal featuring local ingredients.

Plan your visit ↗

24
ART & HISTORY

Visit the Convent of San Domenico in Taggia

The Convent of San Domenico is one of Taggia’s important cultural landmarks, known for its historic setting and works of art. It adds real depth to a visit beyond the beaches and coastal cycle route.

Local tip: Opening and guided-visit arrangements can vary, so check locally before travelling.

Plan your visit ↗

25
BEACH

Spend a beach day in Arma di Taggia

Arma di Taggia is known for its broad sandy shoreline and relaxed seaside atmosphere. It is an easy choice for a classic beach day, especially for families or anyone looking for a long stretch of sand and a promenade close to cafés.

Local tip: In high summer, arrive early if you want a preferred spot; public and managed beach areas have different facilities.

Plan your visit ↗

26
OUTDOORS

Cycle or walk the Riviera dei Fiori coastal path

One of the area's signature experiences is the former railway route transformed into a seaside cycle and walking path. The established Ospedaletti–San Lorenzo al Mare section runs for around 24 kilometres, passing Sanremo, Arma di Taggia, Riva Ligure and Santo Stefano al Mare, with sea views and places to stop along the way.

Local tip: You do not need to do the whole route. Choose a short section, check current access and bike-hire availability, and bring water in warm weather.

Plan your visit ↗

27
HARBOUR & SEASIDE

Pause at Santo Stefano al Mare and Marina degli Aregai

Santo Stefano al Mare combines a small historic seaside centre with the marina at Aregai. It is a pleasant place to stop on a coastal ride or road trip, with a harbour atmosphere and easy access to the waterfront.

Local tip: The marina area and old village have different characters; take a short walk through both.

Plan your visit ↗

28
SEASIDE

Discover Riva Ligure’s small-town rhythm

Riva Ligure is a compact seaside town with a beach, a small historic centre and a relaxed promenade. It is also close to the archaeological site of Costa Balenae, a reminder that this coastline has been a place of settlement and travel for centuries.

Local tip: A good stop for a gentle walk or coffee between Sanremo and San Lorenzo al Mare.

Plan your visit ↗

San Lorenzo al Mare and Imperia

Finish in and around Imperia, where the coast, harbour life and Ligurian food traditions come together.

29
SEASIDE & OUTDOORS

Spend time in San Lorenzo al Mare

San Lorenzo al Mare is a lovely western endpoint for the classic Riviera dei Fiori cycle path, with a small harbour, beaches and a pleasant village centre. It is an easy place to combine a swim, lunch and a stretch of the coastal route.

Local tip: If cycling, check the route status and rental options before setting off, especially outside the main season.

Plan your visit ↗

30
CITY & CULTURE

Discover Imperia’s two sides: Oneglia and Porto Maurizio

Imperia is a city of two historic centres. Oneglia has a working-port feel and strong food traditions, while Porto Maurizio climbs towards the Parasio, a maze of lanes and viewpoints above the sea. Together they make a satisfying final stop on this west-to-east itinerary.

Local tip: Allow time for both districts: they have different atmospheres and are not simply one compact old town.

Plan your visit ↗

Make room for local flavours

Look for Taggiasca olives and extra-virgin olive oil, focaccia, farinata, fresh seafood and the local sardenaira of the Sanremo area. Menus and names vary from town to town, which is part of the pleasure: order what is seasonal and ask what the kitchen recommends.

For a deeper look at the region’s olive-growing tradition, the Museo dell’Olivo in Imperia is a natural addition to a food-focused visit.

Planning your Italian Riviera visit

Where should you base yourself?

For the western end: Ventimiglia and Bordighera work well for the border, gardens and Nervia valley villages. For a lively central base: Sanremo has a broad choice of places to stay, restaurants and rail connections. For the eastern end: Imperia is a good base for exploring Oneglia, Porto Maurizio and the San Lorenzo valley.

When is the best time to visit?

Late spring and early autumn are often ideal for walking, cycling and village-hopping, with warm days and a little less pressure than peak summer. July and August are best for a classic beach holiday but bring more visitors, traffic and full beach clubs. Winter is generally mild by northern European standards, though some gardens, museums, restaurants and seasonal services have reduced opening hours.

Do you need a car?

Not for every part of the coast. The railway connects the main coastal towns, while buses serve some inland villages. A car makes it easier to explore the Nervia and Argentina valleys, reach hilltop villages and combine several small places in one day. Check public-transport timetables carefully for the return journey, particularly in the evening and on Sundays.

How long do you need?

Three days is enough for a taste of Ventimiglia, Bordighera and Sanremo. Five to seven days gives you time to add the inland villages, the cycle path and Imperia without rushing. If you are based locally, the area is also ideal for a series of half-day trips.

Is the Riviera dei Fiori cycle path suitable for beginners?

The established coastal section between Ospedaletti and San Lorenzo al Mare is largely level and can be enjoyed in short sections on foot or by bicycle. It includes tunnels and shared-use areas, so use lights where needed, follow signs and check current route conditions before setting off.

Italian Riviera: frequently asked questions

What is the Italian Riviera between Ventimiglia and Imperia called?

This stretch is commonly associated with the Riviera dei Fiori, or Riviera of Flowers, in the western part of Liguria. The name reflects the area's mild climate, flowers, coastal towns and inland valleys.

What are the prettiest towns near Ventimiglia?

On the coast, Bordighera is a favourite for its old town, gardens and promenade. Inland, Dolceacqua, Apricale, Pigna and Rocchetta Nervina are excellent village visits, each with a different character.

Is Sanremo worth visiting beyond the music festival?

Yes. Sanremo has a historic old town, a Belle Époque seafront, gardens, a casino, markets and easy access to the coastal path. It is a year-round town, not only a festival destination.

Can you explore the western Italian Riviera by train?

Yes. The main coastal towns are connected by rail, making it practical to visit places such as Ventimiglia, Bordighera, Sanremo and Imperia without driving. Inland villages are less convenient by train and may require a bus, taxi or car.

What food should you try in this part of Liguria?

Look for Taggiasca olives and olive oil, focaccia, farinata, fresh seafood, pesto and sardenaira. Local specialities vary across the coast and valleys, so ask about seasonal dishes.

Is the Italian Riviera a good destination outside summer?

Yes. Spring and autumn are especially pleasant for gardens, village walks and cycling, while winter is quieter and often mild. Always check attraction opening times and seasonal transport before travelling.
