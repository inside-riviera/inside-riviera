20 Best Things to Do on a Rainy Day on the Riviera

From aquariums and art museums to perfume workshops, olive-oil tastings and long lunches: make the most of the coast when the weather turns grey.

An independent guide by Inside Riviera · Updated November 2026

The French and Italian Rivieras are famous for sunshine, but a rainy day need not put your plans on hold. In fact, it can be the perfect excuse to explore the region's museums, historic villas, food traditions and creative workshops. You can watch sharks glide through a giant aquarium in Monaco, discover Picasso in an old castle in Antibes, learn how perfume is made in Grasse or trace Liguria's olive-oil heritage in Imperia.

Our rainy-day shortlist

  • Best family outing: the Oceanographic Museum in Monaco
  • Best art day: Nice's museums or the Picasso Museum in Antibes
  • Best hands-on experience: perfume creation in Grasse
  • Best western Liguria experience: the Olive Museum and a Carli food experience in Imperia
  • Best slow afternoon: a spa, a long lunch or a cooking class

The French Riviera: museums, marine life and perfume

Monaco · Families and curious adults

1. Explore the Oceanographic Museum

Built dramatically into the Rock of Monaco, the Oceanographic Museum is one of the coast's strongest rainy-day choices. Allow around two to three hours for aquariums, marine collections and immersive exhibitions. The museum currently notes that its Mediterranean Aquarium is being renovated; other areas, including the tropical aquarium, Great Shark Lagoon, Turtle Odyssey and Mediterranean 2050 exhibition, remain accessible. Check the official site before visiting, especially if a particular aquarium is your priority.

Nice · Art and culture

2. Spend a morning with Matisse or Chagall

Nice has enough museums for several wet days. The Musée Matisse in Cimiez explores the artist's work in a historic villa, while the Musée national Marc Chagall is known for its vivid Biblical Message cycle. Choose one for a relaxed visit or combine them if you have a full day and are happy to travel between them. Check closure days and temporary exhibition changes.

Nice · Historic interiors

3. Discover the story of Nice at Villa Masséna

Just behind the Promenade des Anglais, Villa Masséna offers a look at the city's history in a Belle Époque residence. It is a particularly convenient option if you are staying near the seafront and want to avoid a long journey in bad weather.

Antibes · Art in a castle

4. Visit the Picasso Museum

The Musée Picasso occupies Château Grimaldi in the heart of Antibes old town. The collection includes works Picasso created during his time in Antibes, alongside ceramics and sculpture. Pair it with the nearby covered market or a leisurely lunch. The museum's opening schedule can vary by season and exhibition.

Biot · Glassmaking

5. Watch artisans work with glass

Biot is famous for its bubble glass. A visit to La Verrerie de Biot can turn a rainy afternoon into a craft experience: watch demonstrations when scheduled and browse the glass pieces in the shop. Confirm demonstration times before setting out.

Grasse · Fragrance and history

6. Dive into the world of perfume

The Musée International de la Parfumerie tells the story of fragrance through ingredients, bottles, production techniques and sensory displays. It is an especially good rainy-day destination because the experience is largely indoors. Allow around 90 minutes to two hours, then consider booking a perfume-making workshop with one of Grasse's historic perfume houses.

Cagnes-sur-Mer · History and art

7. Explore the Grimaldi Castle Museum

In Haut-de-Cagnes, the Château-Musée Grimaldi combines historic rooms with art collections and exhibitions. The old village streets around it can be slippery in rain, so wear comfortable shoes and check the museum's current opening schedule.

Saint-Paul-de-Vence · Modern art

8. Visit the Fondation Maeght

The Fondation Maeght is a major modern-art destination, with works by artists including Miró, Giacometti and Chagall. Some of the sculpture garden is outdoors, so it is best for a day of showers or lighter rain rather than a downpour.

Menton · Art and local heritage

9. Discover Jean Cocteau in Menton

The Musée Jean Cocteau – collection Séverin Wunderman and the Bastion museum offer a cultural reason to spend time in Menton. Check which spaces and exhibitions are open before travelling, as museum access and programmes can change.

Cannes · Cinema and shopping

10. Make a cinema day of it

When the rain is persistent, choose a film at one of Cannes' cinemas, browse the covered shopping galleries or settle into a long lunch around the market district. Film screenings and special events can make this a good option for couples, friends and families with older children.

The Italian Riviera: olive oil, maritime history and local culture

Imperia · Food heritage

11. Visit the Carlo Carli Olive Museum

The Museo dell'Olivo Carlo Carli in Imperia explores the long relationship between Liguria and olive oil. Its collection spans cultivation, trade, tools and the place of olive oil in Mediterranean life. The museum's official information recommends checking opening arrangements, and tasting or educational activities may need advance booking. It is one of the most distinctive indoor visits in western Liguria.

Imperia · Maritime history

12. Explore the Naval Museum and Planetarium

The Museo Navale in Porto Maurizio follows Liguria's deep connection with navigation, seafaring and maritime exploration. The planetarium adds an indoor astronomy experience that can work particularly well for families. Opening hours differ by day and the planetarium has its own schedule, so check the official timetable before visiting.

Imperia · Art and unusual collections

13. Visit Villa Faravelli and MACI

Villa Faravelli houses the MACI modern-art collection in an elegant 20th-century villa. It is a quieter alternative to the larger museums on the French coast. Confirm the current exhibition and opening times as access can be limited to selected days.

Imperia · Something different

14. Step into Villa Grock

Villa Grock, the former home of the celebrated clown Grock, is one of Imperia's most unusual cultural attractions. Its history and eccentric architecture make it a memorable visit, but access is by scheduled opening or booking, so do not turn up without checking first.

Sanremo · History and art

15. Discover Sanremo's Civic Museum

The Museo Civico at Palazzo Nota brings together archaeological, artistic and historical collections connected with the city. It is a good way to understand Sanremo beyond its famous music festival and casino. Check the municipality's latest visitor information before planning your visit.

Sanremo · Historic house

16. Visit Villa Nobel

Alfred Nobel's former residence is now a museum dedicated to his life and work. The official visitor information currently lists public opening from Tuesday to Friday, 9:30am–12:30pm, with closure on Mondays, weekends and public holidays. That makes it a morning visit rather than an all-day rainy-day solution. Check current hours and tickets.

Sanremo · Flowers and local identity

17. Learn about the flower-growing tradition

The Floriseum – Museo del Fiore explores the flower-growing history that helped shape Sanremo's identity. It is a small specialist museum, so check the latest schedule before making it the centrepiece of your day.

Perinaldo · Science and local history

18. Look to the stars at the Cassini Museum

The G. D. Cassini Museum in Perinaldo celebrates the astronomer Giovanni Domenico Cassini, who was born in the village. This is a more specialised stop and should be arranged in advance; it is best combined with a meal or a short village visit when the weather eases.

Food, workshops and wellness: the best way to slow down

Both sides of the border · Food lovers

19. Book a cooking class or tasting

A rainy day is ideal for learning to make fresh pasta, pesto, focaccia, ravioli or a Niçoise speciality. Around Imperia, the Fratelli Carli experience offers cooking courses and olive-oil tasting alongside its emporium and museum. In Nice and elsewhere on the French Riviera, look for small-group cooking workshops focused on local dishes. Always reserve ahead and check the language of instruction.

Discover the Carli food and cooking experiences.

Both sides of the border · Relaxation

20. Turn the afternoon into a spa day

Choose a hotel spa, hammam, thermal circuit or massage in Nice, Monaco, Cannes, Menton or Sanremo. Some facilities accept non-residents by appointment, while others are reserved for hotel guests. Ask about day access, treatments and availability before travelling. A spa is often the easiest option when the rain is heavy and you simply want to relax.

Three easy half-day rainy-day itineraries

Nice: art, old-town flavours and coffee

  1. Spend 90 minutes to two hours at the Matisse Museum or Marc Chagall Museum.
  2. Head back towards the centre for lunch or a warm drink in a café.
  3. If time allows, visit Villa Masséna or Palais Lascaris, depending on opening hours.

Best for: couples, art lovers and visitors without a car. Choose one main museum rather than trying to fit in every gallery.

Monaco: an underwater world

  1. Arrive at the Oceanographic Museum near opening time.
  2. Allow two to three hours for the aquarium and exhibitions.
  3. Finish with lunch or a café stop in Monaco-Ville or around Port Hercule.

Best for: families and first-time visitors. Check the aquarium renovation notice before you go.

Sanremo: Nobel, coffee and the old town

  1. Visit Villa Nobel in the morning, Tuesday to Friday, during its published public opening hours.
  2. Stop for coffee and focaccia in central Sanremo.
  3. If it is open, add the Civic Museum or Floriseum; otherwise enjoy a long lunch and browse covered shops.

Best for: a gentle morning in western Liguria. The museum schedules are limited, so check each venue individually.

Three full-day rainy-day itineraries

Grasse: perfume, lunch and a creative workshop

  1. Start at the International Perfume Museum and explore the history of fragrance.
  2. Have lunch in the old town.
  3. Book a perfume-making workshop at a local perfume house for the afternoon.
  4. Allow time for a coffee and a browse through the historic centre between showers.

Best for: couples, friends and anyone who enjoys hands-on experiences. Book the workshop in advance.

Antibes and Biot: Picasso and glassmaking

  1. Begin at the Picasso Museum in Château Grimaldi.
  2. Have lunch in Antibes old town.
  3. Continue to Biot for a glassmaking demonstration or a visit to the Verrerie, if demonstrations are scheduled.
  4. Finish with a coffee or early dinner before heading back.

Best for: art and craft enthusiasts. This route works best with a car or a carefully planned public-transport connection.

Imperia: olive oil, seafaring and Ligurian food

  1. Start at the Carlo Carli Olive Museum and learn about the region's olive-oil culture.
  2. Enjoy lunch in Oneglia or Porto Maurizio, trying local focaccia, pasta or seafood.
  3. Visit the Naval Museum and Planetarium if its timetable matches your afternoon.
  4. Finish with an olive-oil tasting or a food experience, booked in advance.

Best for: food lovers and families who want a distinctly Ligurian day. Check the museums' separate opening schedules before setting out.

Practical tips for a rainy day on the Riviera

  • Check the day, not just the opening hours. Smaller museums often close one or more weekdays, and some Italian venues open only for a morning or selected afternoon sessions.
  • Book workshops ahead. Perfume creation, cooking classes, tastings and spa treatments may require reservations.
  • Group activities geographically. Choose Nice, Monaco, Antibes–Biot, Grasse, Sanremo or Imperia for the day rather than zigzagging along the coast.
  • Leave outdoor attractions for another day. Gardens, hilltop villages and sculpture parks can be slippery or less enjoyable in heavy rain.
  • Use public transport where practical. Nice, Monaco, Antibes and Cannes are well connected by train; inland destinations such as Grasse and Biot need more planning.
  • Keep an eye on weather warnings. Heavy Mediterranean rain can cause localised flooding and transport disruption. Follow official alerts and avoid underpasses, streams and coastal paths during severe weather.
Our advice: On a wet day, plan one main attraction, one good meal and one flexible extra. The Riviera is at its best when you leave time to linger — even when the sea is grey.
