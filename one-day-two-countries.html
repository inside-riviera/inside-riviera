One day.
Two countries.
Endless Riviera.

Why choose between the Italian and French Riviera when you can experience both? Use these flexible ideas to cross the border, discover a different town and enjoy the contrasts along one remarkable coastline.

The idea: explore beyond your base

The coast between Sanremo and Monaco is compact enough to inspire a cross-border day out, but each town has its own character. On the Italian side, you can find Ligurian old-town lanes, markets and a relaxed café culture. Across the border, Menton brings pastel-coloured streets, gardens and a distinctly French Riviera atmosphere.

This guide is designed around experiences rather than a rigid timetable. Choose one route, leave room for a long lunch, and check transport before you set off. You do not need to visit every stop in one day.

Our planning principle: pick one main town in each country. Two places explored properly usually make a better day than four rushed station stops.
Route 1 · Easy-going dayGood for food and wandering

Menton + Ventimiglia

Best for: visitors based around Menton, Ventimiglia or the nearby coast who want a simple taste of both countries.

Morning — Menton, France

  1. Start around the seafront and Les Sablettes, then walk into the old town.
  2. Climb the steps to the square by Saint-Michel Basilica for the view across the bay.
  3. If time allows, browse the covered market or choose one garden or museum rather than trying to fit them all in.

Afternoon — Ventimiglia, Italy

  1. Travel across the border after checking the current rail connection.
  2. Explore the historic centre, with its lanes and viewpoints above the modern town.
  3. Make time for an Italian coffee, focaccia or an unhurried meal before returning.

Why this pairing works: the two towns offer a clear change of atmosphere without turning the day into a race. If your visit falls on a market day, check the current market location and hours before travelling; do not assume every market operates daily.

Route 2 · Seaside and old-town dayGood for a slower pace

Bordighera + Menton

Best for: travellers who like promenades, historic quarters, gardens and time by the sea.

Morning — Bordighera, Italy

  1. Begin with a walk along the seafront and the palm-lined parts of town.
  2. Head up to Bordighera Alta for the quieter historic lanes and a different perspective on the coast.
  3. Stop for lunch or an aperitivo, allowing enough time for the journey west.

Afternoon — Menton, France

  1. Cross via Ventimiglia, checking whether your chosen train requires a change.
  2. Walk the old town towards Saint-Michel Basilica and the viewpoint above the bay.
  3. Finish along the waterfront. If you prefer gardens or museums, choose one and allow time for its opening hours.

Why this pairing works: Bordighera and Menton both reward walking, but their architecture, streets and waterfronts feel different. It is a good route for noticing the character shift as you cross the border.

Route 3 · A fuller Riviera dayGood for confident planners

Sanremo + Monaco

Best for: visitors who want a contrast between a lively Italian coastal city and the compact, grand setting of the Principality of Monaco.

Morning — Sanremo, Italy

  1. Explore La Pigna, the medieval old quarter that climbs above the modern centre.
  2. Walk back towards the seafront and Corso Imperatrice for the Belle Époque side of the city.
  3. Leave time for lunch before travelling west.

Afternoon — Monaco

  1. Check the full journey to Monaco-Monte-Carlo station and allow for any connection at Ventimiglia.
  2. Choose one main area: Monte-Carlo and Casino Square, or Monaco-Ville on the Rock. Trying to cover everything in a short visit can feel rushed.
  3. Use the public lifts and escalators where helpful, but check local access and opening information on the day.

Why this pairing works: it brings together two very different Riviera identities. It is the most ambitious of these three suggestions, so check return options before you leave and consider reversing the order if that better suits your starting point.

How to plan the border crossing

Check the whole journey, not just the first train

Rail services, engineering works and connections can change. Compare the outward and return journeys for your actual date, especially when travelling between the French TER network and Italian regional services. A journey that looks direct on a map may require a change at Ventimiglia.

Keep these practical details in mind

  • Documents: carry the valid identity or travel documents required for your nationality and circumstances, even when crossing an internal Schengen border.
  • Last return: check the last practical train in both directions before committing to a late lunch or evening event.
  • Market days: market schedules can vary by town, season and public holiday. Confirm locally before making a market the centrepiece of your day.
  • Accessibility: old towns often have steep streets, steps and uneven paving. Check station facilities and the walking route if step-free access matters.
  • Driving: allow extra time for coastal traffic and check parking and restricted-traffic zones before entering historic centres.
  • Opening hours: museums, gardens and attractions may have seasonal hours or closure days. Check the official venue information.
A note about timings: this is an itinerary guide, not a live timetable. We have deliberately not quoted fixed journey times or ticket prices because these vary by date, service and disruption. Always verify before departure.
